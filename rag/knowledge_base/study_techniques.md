# Study Techniques

## Study breaks and focus intervals
Long, unbroken study sessions tend to reduce focus. Many students work in focused blocks of about 25 to 50 minutes followed by a short 5 to 10 minute break, and take a longer break after a few blocks. During breaks, stepping away from the screen, stretching, drinking water or walking briefly helps more than switching to another screen.

## Active recall
Testing yourself is usually more effective than rereading. Close the notes and try to write or say what you remember, answer practice questions, or explain the topic aloud as if teaching someone. Then check what you missed and focus on those gaps.

## Spaced repetition
Reviewing material several times spread over days works better for long-term memory than cramming it all at once. A simple schedule is to review new material the next day, again a few days later, and again the following week.

## When you can't remember anything
Feeling like nothing is sticking is common, especially when tired or stressed. It often helps to stop, take a real break, then switch to a smaller goal: pick one short section, use active recall on just that part, and build up from there. Sleep plays an important role in consolidating what you studied.

## Planning exam preparation
Break the syllabus into small topics and spread them across the days before the exam. Put the hardest or least familiar topics earlier, leave time for past papers, and keep the final day for light review rather than new material.
